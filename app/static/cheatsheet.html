The Demo's Rational

What I built, why it matters.

Use this as the first-person narrative. Show the proof-point pages for context and move into the working system only when demonstrating evidence.

30-second opening

I identified an opportunity to improve how lawyers, partners and staff get support and use AI in day-to-day legal work. The ambition is better legal quality, stronger commercial performance and better-prepared court work. Those are aspirations, not results I am claiming. I designed and implemented this POC to show how ABL could test them responsibly, measure actual outcomes and stop investing when the evidence does not support continuation.

The central evidence boundary

Aspiration

Improve accepted legal work, realised economics and the quality of court preparation.

What this POC proves

A controlled operating model: sources, prompts, skills, human authority, measures, cost oversight and stop rules.

What a pilot must prove

Material improvement against an agreed ABL baseline using representative work and lawyer-reviewed results.

The six-part narrative

What I identified

Matter and IT requests can involve repeated chasing, inconsistent diagnosis, unclear ownership and avoidable rework. AI initiatives can also become disconnected pilots without a controlled improvement lifecycle.

What I designed

One operating pattern: frame the need, gather permitted evidence, prepare a structured proposal, enforce deterministic controls, retain named human authority and measure the accepted outcome.

What I implemented

A six-case support desk, four Harvey legal-work patterns, reusable prompts and skills, prompt-version regression, approval-gated activation, audit trails, scorecards and review definitions.

What the working system does

It processes synthetic tickets, checks evidence and controls, stops unsafe actions, records approvals, evaluates Harvey work patterns and compares prompt versions before release.

What evidence it produces

The support desk produces case outcomes and control results. The Harvey review proves each use case has sources, an output contract, lawyer review, measures and stops before a controlled pilot; it does not invent outcome evidence.

My operating model and release rules

Harvest, version, evaluate, approve, monitor and reconsider. Continue, change, replace, roll back, retire or stop according to quality, safety, useful adoption, relevance and total cost.

Matter-opening proof

The matter is approved, but the lawyer cannot find it or record time. The POC does not guess or blindly retry. It finds the request, checks authoritative evidence, determines whether this is creation, reconciliation or recovery, stops unsafe actions and routes a genuine exception to the accountable owner.
Reads the request

Uses the stable ticket and intake identifiers instead of relying on free-text interpretation alone.

Checks evidence

Verifies approval, reference data, target lookup and processing state from authoritative sources.

Distinguishes the action

Chooses controlled creation, reconciliation of an existing matter or lookup-before-retry recovery.

Stops unsafe actions

Blocks bad master data, restricted access, unknown live-model cost and unauthorised execution.

Routes exceptions

Sends the evidence-backed issue to the reference-data, information-barrier or service owner.

Measures the outcome

Tracks ready-to-work time, first-time-right completion, chasing and harmful shortcuts.

Release rules

Activate

Only after the agreed evaluation passes and the named owner approves.

Change

When corrections, user friction or changed sources reveal a better pattern.

Replace

When a rule, search, knowledge article or conventional automation is safer or cheaper.

Retire or stop

When relevance disappears, cost exceeds value, or privacy, privilege, safety or reliability fails.

Useful language and claims to avoid

Say

  • “I built the support POC…”
  • “What I designed…”
  • “What I implemented…”
  • “My operating model…”
  • “My release rule…”
  • “The system proof is…”
  • “This measures better preparation and accepted work.”

Avoid

  • “AI will win more cases.”
  • “Minutes saved equal profit.”
  • “This represents ABL’s current configuration.”
  • “The model makes the access or legal decision.”
  • Unsupported percentage benefits without a baseline.
  • Describing review definitions as already scheduled production automations.

Close

The point is not to add AI everywhere. It is to create a repeatable way to identify useful opportunities, implement them with controls, reuse what works and stop spending when the evidence no longer supports the capability.
